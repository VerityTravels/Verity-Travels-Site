Trip Protection

What Travel Insurance Actually Covers (And Why It's Worth the Add)

4 min read · Planning & Protection

Travel insurance is the add-on nobody thinks about until the moment they really, really wish they'd bought it. It's easy to skip when everything is going according to plan — which is exactly the problem, since plans are the thing that stop going right.

Here's what actually tends to go wrong on trips, and what a solid policy is built to handle when it does.

The Uh-Ohs That Actually Happen

  • The connection you miss by ten minutes One delayed first flight, one missed connection, and suddenly you're rebooking a cruise embarkation or losing the first night of a hotel stay you already paid for.
  • The illness that shows up two weeks before departure A sudden diagnosis, a family emergency, a job that suddenly can't spare you the week — the kind of thing that forces a cancellation with zero notice.
  • The stomach bug on day four Getting sick or hurt somewhere your regular insurance doesn't reach, and needing actual medical care — or transportation to somewhere that can provide it.
  • The bag that doesn't make the connection Lost, delayed, or damaged luggage — with everything you packed for the trip stuck somewhere you aren't.
  • The passport that goes missing Lost travel documents abroad turn into a multi-day scramble without help navigating the local consulate process.

What Good Coverage Actually Covers

Policies vary, but a solid whole-trip plan generally includes:

CategoryWhat It Typically Covers
Trip cancellationReimbursement for non-refundable costs if you have to cancel for a covered reason before departure.
Trip interruptionCoverage if the trip is cut short partway through — often at a higher reimbursement rate than cancellation.
Emergency medicalTreatment abroad for a sudden illness or injury, including hospitalization and prescriptions.
Medical evacuationTransportation to the nearest appropriate facility for a serious medical event.
Baggage & belongingsReimbursement for lost, delayed, or stolen luggage and personal items.
Delay & missed connectionReimbursement for meals, hotels, or rebooking costs when a delay causes a missed connection.
Cancel for any reason (add-on)Broader cancellation flexibility beyond the standard covered-reason list, usually reimbursed at a lower percentage.

Timing matters more than people expect. Buying within about two weeks of your initial trip deposit is often what unlocks coverage for pre-existing medical conditions and eligibility for "cancel for any reason" upgrades. Wait too long, and those options can disappear even if you still buy a policy.
