A Scandinavia & Russia Cruise: What Actually Surprised Me

Royal Caribbean, Serenade of the Seas · 7 nights · 5 min read

Gamla Stan, Stockholm — one of the stops that stuck with me most.

Stockholm is the port I still think about. It was gone 11pm and the sky hadn't committed to dark — just a long, pale blue that made the whole city feel wide awake. Between that and how unbelievably clean and modern everything felt, it didn't feel like any other port I'd sailed into.

I booked this one expecting a fairly standard Northern Europe itinerary. What I got was one of the more distinct week-long sails I've done — culturally dense, visually nothing like the Caribbean, and full of small surprises. A few of them are worth knowing before you book this itinerary yourself.

Stockholm Ruins You a Little

Sailing in July means arriving to nearly round-the-clock daylight — the kind where you step off the ship at 9pm and it still looks like late afternoon. It changes the whole rhythm of a port day; you're not racing the sunset back to the ship, you're just... still out.

And then there was dinner. I had farmed reindeer that night and it's, without exaggeration, some of the best meat I've ever eaten — nothing like what the description prepares you for. It's the kind of thing you'd never think to seek out on your own, and exactly the kind of local specific a good itinerary should point you toward instead of leaving you at the ship's buffet.

The advisor lens This is the detail people underestimate about Scandinavia sailings: the food and the light are as much the experience as the sightseeing. A good itinerary leaves room for both — you don't want to be rushed back to the ship right when the city is at its best.

What This Itinerary Is Actually For

A Scandinavia & Russia route isn't a relaxation cruise — it's closer to a moving European history tour. The pace is different from a Caribbean sailing: more full days ashore, more walking, more time in cities built centuries before the ship that brought you there. If your idea of a cruise is sea days and a pool deck, this isn't that trip. If you want a week that feels like several distinct countries stitched together, it's hard to beat.

Booking It Well

  • Go in summer. June through August is when you get the long daylight that makes port days feel unhurried — sail this in shoulder season and you lose a lot of what makes it memorable.
  • Take the balcony. The approach into Stockholm through the archipelago is worth watching from your own railing, not through a public-deck crowd.
  • Plan port days around food, not just sights. The museum will be there next time. The reindeer might not be on the menu the same way twice.
A note on Russia stops: Baltic and Russia itineraries have shifted since 2022, and St. Petersburg calls that used to be standard on this route aren't guaranteed on current sailings. If a Scandinavia cruise is on your list, it's worth confirming the actual current port list before booking — which is exactly the kind of detail we check before recommending an itinerary.
