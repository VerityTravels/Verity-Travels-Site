Why I Always Book Day Flights to London (And You Might Want To)

4 min read · Updated 2026

Most people crossing the Atlantic default to the overnight flight without thinking twice about it. I almost never book one — and after enough trips doing it the other way, I don't think I ever will again.

A daytime flight to London sounds counterintuitive at first. You lose a night of "sleeping through" the flight, and you land in the evening instead of the morning. But that's exactly the point.

The Case for Flying During the Day

An overnight flight promises sleep it rarely delivers. You land jet-lagged, running on broken airplane sleep, and immediately have to function in a new time zone — usually while checking into a hotel room that isn't ready yet.

A day flight flips that. You land in the evening, check into a hotel that's actually ready for you, have dinner, and go to sleep at a normal local bedtime. Your body adjusts to the new time zone almost immediately, because you're not fighting exhaustion and confusion at the same time.

It's Easier on Everyone, Especially Kids

This matters even more traveling with children. Asking a kid to sleep upright on a plane, then function through a full day of sightseeing on no real rest, is asking a lot. A day flight means they watch movies, eat plane snacks, and arrive tired in the normal, end-of-a-long-day way — not the overtired, overstimulated way that turns into a rough first night.

The Money Angle Nobody Talks About

Day flights to London are also frequently the better deal. They tend to be less popular than the red-eye, which usually means:

  • Cheaper fares — less demand for the daytime slot often means better pricing than the overnight option on the same route.
  • Better upgrade space — lighter loads on day flights mean more availability if you're using miles or hoping for a complimentary upgrade.
  • Lighter flights overall — more room to spread out, even in economy.
How I actually do it Fly during the day into London, get a hotel, and adjust immediately — dinner, normal bedtime, done. The next day, continue onward to the final destination on a budget carrier. It splits what would be one long, rough travel day into two easy ones.

How to Actually Build the Trip

The move isn't just the flight — it's the full sequence. Fly during the day, land in London, and treat that first night as a real stop, not a layover. Get a hotel near the airport or in the city, have a proper dinner, and go to sleep at a normal local hour. The next morning, you're already adjusted, and a short hop on a budget carrier gets you to the final destination without stacking a long-haul flight and a connection into the same exhausting day.

It adds a night to the trip, but it trades one miserable day of travel for two manageable ones — and you usually save money doing it.
