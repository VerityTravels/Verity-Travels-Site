Best Time to Book a Royal Caribbean Alaska Cruise (And When to Avoid It)

5 min read · Updated 2026

Most people book their Alaska cruise the same way they'd book anywhere else — whenever it's convenient. That's usually the first mistake.

Alaska is one of the only cruise destinations with a real season, not just a range of good and bad weather. The month you sail changes daylight hours, wildlife activity, glacier conditions, and pricing — sometimes dramatically. Here's how to think about timing before you book.

Alaska Actually Has a Season

Most Caribbean or Mediterranean itineraries sail comfortably across a wide window of the year. Alaska is different. The cruise season runs roughly from late April through September, and where you land inside that window shapes almost everything about the trip — not just the temperature.

Timing What to Expect
Late April–Late May Lower fares, thinner crowds, longer daylight arriving fast. Cooler temperatures and a slightly higher chance of rain.
June–Early August Peak wildlife season and the longest daylight of the year, but also peak pricing and the busiest ports.
Late August–September Fall colors starting inland, fewer crowds, and a real chance at the Northern Lights on clear nights — paired with bigger temperature swings and a shorter booking window as the season winds down.

The Itinerary Matters as Much as the Month

Glacier viewing, wildlife sightings, and port stops in Alaska are far more weather-dependent than a typical Caribbean itinerary, where the ship's schedule rarely changes regardless of conditions. A glacier that's spectacular in June can look completely different by September, and wildlife sightings shift with the season as animals move between feeding grounds.

Before booking, it's worth checking what a specific itinerary is actually built around — some sailings emphasize glacier viewing and scenic cruising days, while others are more port-heavy with time built in for excursions.

Book Early for the Cabins That Actually Matter

On a scenic itinerary like Alaska, cabin placement changes the trip in a way it doesn't on most other routes. The best balcony cabins for glacier viewing — positioned on the correct side of the ship for the scenic cruising days — sell out first, often months before departure.

The pattern worth remembering Booking early in Alaska isn't just about price. It's about securing the specific cabin placement that makes the scenic days worth what you paid for them.

The Mistake Most First-Timers Make

The most common mistake isn't picking the wrong month — it's picking a month based on price alone, without checking what that trade-off actually costs in daylight, wildlife activity, or weather reliability. A cheaper September sailing can be a great trip, but only if you know what you're trading for that lower fare.
